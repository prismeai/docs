title: 'How access control works'

---

Prisme.ai decides what each person, API key, and automation can do through role-based access control (RBAC). Access is not set in one place: four layers each answer a different question, are managed by a different person, and are configured on a different screen. This page explains the layers, how a request is evaluated against them, and where to configure each one.

## The four layers

| Layer | Question it answers | Who manages it | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Platform role](#platform-roles) | Can this person administer the whole platform? | Platform administrator | Infrastructure configuration, or **Platform** in Governance |
| [Organization role](#organization-roles) | Which products can this member open, and which actions can they take in them? | Organization administrator | Governance, **Roles** and **Members** |
| [Resource sharing](#resource-sharing) | Which agents, knowledge bases, and collections can this member reach? | Owner of the resource | The **Share** or **Sharing** dialog of the resource |
| [Workspace role](#workspace-roles) | What can this person do inside one Builder workspace and the apps it serves? | Workspace owner | Builder, workspace **Settings**, **Sharing** and **Security** tabs |

The layers are independent. An organization **Admin** has no access to a Builder workspace until someone shares it with them, and a workspace owner has no organization role because of it.

### Platform roles

A platform role applies across every organization. There are three:

| Platform role | Grants |
|---|---|
| `superadmin` | Full access to every organization and every workspace. |
| `root` | Full access to every organization, but no access to workspaces, pages, or events. |
| `org-creator` | Permission to create organizations, and nothing else at platform level. |

Most users have no platform role. To grant one, see [Configure platform administrators](/self-hosting/entreprise/super-admins).

### Organization roles

An [organization](/products/ai-governance/overview#organizations) is the tenant boundary around members and product resources. Each member holds one role in each organization they belong to, and only the role of the organization they are active in applies. Switching organizations changes which products and resources they see.

A role is a list of **permissions** written `product:resource:action`, such as `agent-factory:agents:write`, with optional **scopes** that restrict it to specific resources, such as one agent. Every organization starts with system roles (**Owner**, **Admin**, **Member**, **Agent Maker**, **Builder**) and can define its own.

- To look up permissions, scopes, and the system roles, see [Organization roles and permissions](/products/access-control/organization-roles).
- To create a role or change a member's role, see [Create and assign organization roles](/products/access-control/manage-organization-roles).

### Resource sharing

Agents, knowledge bases, and collections each carry their own access list. Their owner shares them with a user, a group, or the whole organization, under a product role such as **Reader**, **Editor**, **Admin**, or **Owner**. Sharing is how a member reaches a resource that their organization role does not scope them to.

Sharing never replaces the organization role. See [How the layers combine](#how-the-layers-combine) below, and the sharing page of each product:

- [Agent Factory: sharing and access](/products/agent-factory/settings#sharing-%26-access)
- [Knowledges: sharing and access control](/products/ai-knowledge/sharing)

### Workspace roles

A Builder workspace is shared person by person, from its **Sharing** tab, with the built-in `owner` or `editor` role or a role the workspace declares. The workspace security rules, written in YAML in its **Security** tab, define custom roles, grant them actions on pages, files, events, and automations, assign them automatically to users of an identity provider, and control what anonymous visitors and API keys can do.

- To share a workspace and write its rules, see [Control access to a workspace](/products/access-control/workspace-roles).
- To look up subjects, actions, and rule syntax, see [Workspace security rules reference](/products/access-control/workspace-rules-reference).

## How the layers combine

When a request reaches a product such as Agent Factory, Knowledges, or Collections, the platform evaluates it in this order:

1. **Authentication.** The caller is identified by a user session, a personal access token, an organization API key, or a service account token. An unidentified caller is refused with `Unauthorized`.
2. **Platform role.** A `superadmin` or `root` caller is allowed without further checks.
3. **Organization permission.** The role in the active organization must hold the permission for the action, for example `agent-factory:agents:write` to edit an agent. Without it, the request is refused with `Access denied: missing permission '<product>:<resource>:<action>'`, whatever the resource is shared with.
4. **Resource access.** The caller must then reach the specific resource through one of these paths:
   - a wildcard scope on the role, such as `*` or `agent-factory:agents:*`, which reaches every resource of that type;
   - a scope naming the resource, such as `agent-factory:agents:agent_abc123`;
   - a share (a binding) to the caller, to one of their groups, or to their organization, whose product role includes the action.

So the two layers multiply: the organization role decides **which actions** a member may take at all, and scopes or shares decide **on which resources**. Granting **Editor** on one agent does not let a **Member** edit it, because the **Member** role does not hold `agent-factory:agents:write`.

When several shares reach the same resource (one to the user, one to a group, one to the organization), the most privileged one applies. A narrower share never removes access that a broader one grants.

<Note>
Products can add rules of their own on top of this check. For example, the owner of an agent always reaches it, and a public, published agent is readable by everyone. The sharing page of each product documents these rules.
</Note>

Builder workspaces follow a separate path: a request on a workspace, its pages, files, events, or automations is evaluated against the workspace roles and security rules. The organization role is not consulted; only the `superadmin` platform role reaches every workspace.

## Choose where to grant access

| You want to | Do this |
|---|---|
| Let a member create agents and knowledge bases | Give them the **Agent Maker** role. See [Create and assign organization roles](/products/access-control/manage-organization-roles). |
| Let a member build automations and apps | Give them the **Builder** role, then share the workspaces they work on. See [Control access to a workspace](/products/access-control/workspace-roles). |
| Restrict a role to a few agents or knowledge bases | Add scopes to a custom role. See [Create and assign organization roles](/products/access-control/manage-organization-roles#restrict-a-role-to-specific-resources). |
| Give one person access to one agent or knowledge base | Share the resource with them from its sharing dialog. |
| Give a team the same access | Create a [group](/products/ai-governance/identity-access#groups) and share resources with it. |
| Let end users open a Builder app | Assign workspace roles from **Sharing**. See [Role-based access for your app](/products/ai-builder/spa-access-control). |
| Restrict who can call an automation | See [Restrict who can run an automation](/products/access-control/protect-automations). |
| Give a script or an automation API access to a workspace | See [Create workspace API keys](/products/access-control/workspace-api-keys). |
| Give a machine access to organization products | Create an [organization API key](/products/ai-governance/identity-access#api-keys) or a [service account](/products/ai-governance/identity-access#service-accounts). |
| Control which tools an agent may call without approval | See [Tool permissions](/products/agent-factory/tool-permissions). |

If a member is refused access, see [Troubleshoot access denied errors](/products/access-control/troubleshooting).
